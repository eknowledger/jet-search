# Reading Notes — Thinking in Systems

## The core idea

A system is a set of parts connected so that together they produce a pattern of behaviour over time. You understand it by its stocks (what accumulates), its flows (what fills and drains the stocks) and the feedback loops between them, not by its parts alone.

## Concepts worth keeping

- **Balancing loops** push a stock towards a goal: a thermostat, a hungry person eating.
- **Reinforcing loops** amplify: compound interest, a rumour, erosion.
- **Delays** in a loop cause oscillation. Most of the "overreaction" we see in markets and in teams is a delay problem.
- **Bounded rationality**: each actor makes sensible choices with the information they have, and the sum is still a mess.

## Leverage points

The book ranks places to intervene in a system, from weakest to strongest. Changing numbers (taxes, quotas) is near the bottom. Changing the rules, the information flows, and above all the goals and the mindset behind the system sits at the top. The surprise is how much effort usually goes into the weak end.
