# Eating for training

Not a diet. Just enough structure that long runs don't feel like running on empty and recovery is quick. Recipes live in [[Cooking]].

## Every day

- Protein at every meal — eggs, beans, fish, yoghurt. Roughly a palm-sized portion.
- Vegetables at lunch and dinner; the [[Rooftop Garden]] tomatoes covered most of the summer.
- Vitamin D, 2,000 IU, with breakfast ([[Blood test 2026-09]]).
- Water: a big bottle on the kitchen table, two a day.

## Long-run days (Sunday)

| When | What |
|---|---|
| the night before | rice or pasta with something simple; nothing new |
| 2 h before | sourdough toast with honey, a banana, coffee, water |
| during (runs over 75 min) | a gel at km 8; water |
| within an hour after | chocolate milk, or yoghurt and fruit — then a real lunch |

## Quality days (Tuesday)

- A small snack 60–90 minutes before: a banana or a slice of sourdough with jam.
- Dinner after: carbs and protein — bacalhau com grão is the classic.

## Race week

- Last two days: more carbs (around 7–8 g per kg), less fibre. White rice, pasta, bread from Fermento's loaves, potatoes.
- Race morning: the long-run breakfast, 3–4 hours before the start.
- In the race, gels at **km 8** and **km 15** — see [[Race day plan]].

## Things I learned

- Coffee before a long run: yes. Two coffees: no.
- Too much fibre the night before (bean stew, 2026-08-22): never again before a long run.
- Sourdough toast with honey is the perfect pre-run food. Fermento, the starter, finally pulls its weight.

## Simple go-to meals

- Overnight oats with grated apple and cinnamon.
- Pasta with sauce from the frozen garden tomatoes, plus tuna.
- Rice, salmon, greens — the race-eve dinner.
- Grão com espinafres (chickpeas with spinach).
- Omelette with whatever herbs survived on the terrace.
