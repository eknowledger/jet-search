# Braiding Sweetgrass

**Robin Wall Kimmerer** · finished March 2026 · ★★★★★ · [[Book Club]] April pick (Carla)

Kimmerer is a botanist and a member of the Citizen Potawatomi Nation, and the book braids her two ways of knowing — science and Indigenous teaching — around plants: sweetgrass, pecans, strawberries, maples, the Three Sisters. I read it slowly, one essay a night, over six weeks.

## What I thought
The gentlest book that ever changed my mind. The central idea is reciprocity: the land gives gifts, and a gift creates a relationship and an obligation, not a transaction. Most of my gardening was a transaction — I buy compost, I get tomatoes. This made me feel faintly rude.

The essay on the Three Sisters — corn, beans and squash grown together, each helping the others — is the one I reread. The beans climb the corn and feed the soil with nitrogen; the squash shades the ground for both. There's no room for corn on an 18 m² roof, but that idea is why the [[Winter plan — broad beans and garlic]] puts the broad beans in the tomato trough: the beans give back to the soil what the tomatoes took.

The Honorable Harvest — never take the first one, never take more than half, use everything, share, give thanks — is written on a card and taped to the lid of the [[Compost]] bin.

## Quotes
> "All flourishing is mutual."

> "Restoration is a powerful antidote to despair."

## What it changed
- I compost properly now, and I think of it as giving back rather than throwing away.
- I say thank you to the lemon tree. Out loud. Only when nobody's on the neighbouring roofs.
