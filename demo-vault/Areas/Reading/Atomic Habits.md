# Atomic Habits

**James Clear** · finished January 2026 · ★★★

The New Year book. Read it in the first week of January with a pencil, which is the only way to read a book like this.

## The ideas that stuck
1. **Systems over goals.** The goal is the result; the system is what you do every day. Everyone who fails also had a goal.
2. **Identity-based habits.** Don't aim to "write a novel"; become someone who writes before breakfast. Every morning is a piece of evidence.
3. **Habit stacking.** "After [current habit], I will [new habit]." Mine: after I pour the coffee, I sit down and write.
4. **Make it easy.** The two-minute rule: start so small you can't say no. Then just show up.
5. **Never miss twice.** One miss is an accident; two is the start of a different habit.

## Quotes
> "You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."

> "Every action you take is a vote for the type of person you wish to become."

## What I actually did with it
- The [[Writing habit]] — 600 words a day before breakfast, Sunday revision — is this book applied. It started on 1 June and it has held better than anything I've tried before.
- Running shoes by the door the night before. For the [[Half Marathon]] block from November, the plan goes on the back of the bedroom door, where I can't avoid it.
- Phone charging in the hall, not by the bed. My [[Reading]] went up immediately.

## Why only three stars
It could have been a long essay. Every idea gets four anecdotes, and most of the anecdotes are about famous winners — nobody tells you about the people with perfect systems who still didn't make it. And the tone is relentlessly upbeat, like a very kind coach who won't stop clapping.

But it changed what I do every morning. That's more than most five-star books manage.
