# To the Lighthouse

**Virginia Woolf** · reading · [[Book Club]] November pick (mine), meeting on 2026-11-05

The Ramsays and their guests at a summer house on the Isle of Skye, and a trip to the lighthouse across the bay that a small boy desperately wants to make — and doesn't, for ten years.

## Why now
Partly for the club, mostly for [[The Lighthouse Keeper]]. I'd avoided it for years because I was afraid it would make my book look silly. It does, a bit. That's fine.

## Where I am
Into the last part, "The Lighthouse", with about forty pages to go. "Time Passes" — the short middle section where ten years go by in twenty pages, the house empties, the war happens, and people die in brackets — is the most astonishing thing I've read this year. It's exactly what I need for the gap between Part Two and Part Three of my book: how to let time pass without explaining it.

## Quotes so far
> "For nothing was simply one thing."

> "The great revelation perhaps never did come. Instead there were little daily miracles, illuminations, matches struck unexpectedly in the dark."

## Notes as I go
- The lighthouse itself is almost never described. It's a thing people look at and mean something by. Mine is the opposite — all paraffin and gears and mercury ([[Research — lighthouses of the Azores]]). Maybe that's my answer to Woolf.
- Mrs Ramsay watches the long, steady stroke of the beam at night and feels it's hers. Elena does something like that already. Not stealing; noticing.
- The sentences are long, and you have to stop resisting them.
