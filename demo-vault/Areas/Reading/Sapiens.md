# Sapiens

**Yuval Noah Harari** · finished August 2026 · ★★★ · [[Book Club]] September pick (Duarte)

The whole history of our species in about 450 pages: the cognitive revolution, the agricultural revolution, the unification of humankind through money, empires and religion, and the scientific revolution. I read most of it on the terrace in the August heat, which may have coloured my mood.

## What I thought
Brilliant in the first half, glib in the second. The big idea is great and has stayed with me: humans can cooperate in huge numbers because we believe in shared fictions — gods, nations, money, companies, human rights. None of them exist outside our heads; all of them run the world.

The agricultural revolution chapter is the best. Wheat as the winner, and us as its servants.

After that it gets breezy. Big claims, thin evidence, and a habit of saying "most historians agree" when they don't. By the chapters on happiness I was arguing in the margins like Duarte.

## Quotes
> "We did not domesticate wheat. It domesticated us."

> "History began when humans invented gods, and will end when humans become gods."

## Useful for
- [[The Lighthouse Keeper]]: a lighthouse is a shared fiction too. Two flashes every ten seconds means "Ponta Negra" only because every ship has agreed that it does. When the clock stops, the fiction breaks — that's chapter 12.
- [[Finances]]: money as a story everyone agrees to believe. Strangely comforting while staring at the emergency fund.

## Book Club verdict
Our lowest score ever, 2.9, and our longest meeting. Duarte defended every page. Carla had read the critics. Mariana said she just wanted to know what happened to the Neanderthals. Best night of the year.
