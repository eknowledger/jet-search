# The Remains of the Day

**Kazuo Ishiguro** · finished March 2026 · ★★★★★ · [[Book Club]] March pick (Joana)

Stevens, an English butler, takes a few days' motoring trip to the West Country in 1956 to visit Miss Kenton, the housekeeper who left Darlington Hall twenty years before. On the way he tells us about service, about "dignity", about Lord Darlington — and tells us, without ever quite saying it, everything he got wrong.

## What I thought
A perfect book. Every sentence is polite and every sentence is hiding something. Stevens never lies to us; he just never looks straight at the thing, and the reader sees it over his shoulder: the father he didn't sit with while he was dying, Lord Darlington's sympathy for the Nazis, and Miss Kenton, whom he loved and let go.

I read it straight after *Never Let Me Go*, then [[Klara and the Sun]] in April. The three together teach the same lesson: a narrator who serves, and what the serving costs.

## Quotes
> "Indeed — why should I not admit it? — at that moment, my heart was breaking."

> "The evening's the best part of the day."

The second is a stranger on the pier at Weymouth, near the end. I underlined it twice.

## For my own writing
This is the book for [[Tomás Varga]]: a proud man whose whole self is his duty, losing it and unable to say so. The trick is restraint — let the reader feel what the character won't admit. It's also why [[Chapter 7 — Dona Graça]] is in first person: Graça is a narrator who tells you everything except the one thing.

## Book Club verdict
Group average 4.1. Joana read the pier scene aloud and nobody spoke for a while. Then Mariana said she wanted to slap Stevens, and Beatriz said that was love too.
