# Klara and the Sun

**Kazuo Ishiguro** · finished April 2026 · ★★★★ · [[Book Club]] May pick (Mariana)

Klara is an Artificial Friend — a robot companion in a shop window — chosen by a sick girl called Josie. Everything is told by Klara, who sees the world in boxes and segments, believes the Sun is a kind of god who heals people, and understands much less, and much more, than the humans around her.

## What I thought
My third Ishiguro this year, after *Never Let Me Go* and [[The Remains of the Day]], and you can feel it's the same writer: a narrator whose whole existence is to serve, telling a story whose real meaning they can't quite see. Stevens serves a house; Klara serves a child. Both break your heart with what they don't say.

The world-building is done almost entirely by leaving things out. "Lifted" children, a "substitution", a society shaped by something nobody explains. It drove Duarte mad. I loved it — you learn the world the way Klara does, in fragments.

Klara's faith in the Sun is the part I keep coming back to. It's childish and it's wrong, and the book refuses to laugh at it.

## Quote
> "There was something very special, but it wasn't inside Josie. It was inside those who loved her."

## For my own writing
- A narrator with a limited way of seeing makes the reader do half the seeing.
- Dona Graça in [[Chapter 7 — Dona Graça]] owes something to Klara: a narrator who watches everyone and is never asked what she wants.
- Light as faith — Klara and the Sun, Elena and the lamp in [[The Lighthouse Keeper]]. Careful not to borrow too much.

## Book Club verdict
Group average 3.7. Mariana cried. Duarte called Klara "a very sad toaster". Carla said that was the point.
