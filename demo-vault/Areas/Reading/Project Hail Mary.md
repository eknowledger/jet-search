# Project Hail Mary

**Andy Weir** · finished January 2026 · ★★★★★

Read it in three days over New Year, mostly on the sofa under a blanket with Pimenta on my feet. The most fun I've had with a book in years.

## What I thought
A schoolteacher wakes up alone on a spaceship with no memory of how he got there and his two crewmates dead in their bunks. The science *is* the plot: every chapter is a problem, and he solves it with arithmetic, educated guesses and a lot of swearing. It shouldn't be moving. It is.

Then Rocky arrives and the book turns into something else — a friendship between two beings with no language in common who build one, word by word, out of musical notes and maths. Those chapters were my favourite. It's exactly the feeling of learning a language from zero: the first sentence that lands, the first joke that gets a laugh on both sides. I thought about it a lot while grinding through katakana ([[Languages]]).

## Quotes
> "Fist my bump."

> "Amaze! Amaze! Amaze!"

Both Rocky. [[Rui]] now says the second one whenever dinner is good.

## What stayed with me
- Competence as a kind of kindness. Ryland is good at things, and he uses it to help.
- The flashbacks slowly reveal he isn't quite the hero he thinks he is. That twist is what makes the ending land.
- Rocky ends his questions with "question?" — a tiny grammar decision that makes him a person.

## Quibbles
- The memory loss is convenient.
- The prose is plain to the point of invisibility. For this book, that's a strength.

Passed it on to Rui, who finished it in two days and then wanted to talk about xenonite for a week.
