# Pachinko

**Min Jin Lee** · finished February 2026 · ★★★★★ · [[Book Club]] February pick (Beatriz)

Four generations of a Korean family, from a fishing village near Busan in 1910 to Osaka and Tokyo in 1989. Sunja, the daughter of a boarding-house family, gets pregnant by a rich married man, marries a gentle minister instead, and moves with him to Japan, where her family will be foreigners for eighty years.

## What I thought
I read it in a week and then sat with it for much longer. It's an old-fashioned saga in the best sense — you simply follow people's lives, year after year, and history happens to them: the occupation, the war, the long humiliation of being Korean in Japan, the pachinko parlours as one of the few trades open to them.

And one of the sons is called **Noa**. I wasn't ready for what happens to him. Beatriz says she chose the book partly for that; I'm still deciding whether to forgive her.

## Quote
> "History has failed us, but no matter."

The opening line, and maybe the whole book.

## What stayed with me
- Leaving is never one decision. Sunja leaves Korea once and spends the rest of her life not quite arriving. That's in [[The Lighthouse Keeper]] now: Elena thinks leaving is a door, and it's a corridor.
- Hansu, the rich man, is the most interesting character — kind, ruthless, always there.
- The women carry everything. Kimchi sold on the street keeps the family alive through the war.

## For Japan
A lot of the book happens in Osaka, in Ikaino, the old Korean district. We have two nights in Osaka on the [[Japan Trip 2027]]; I want to walk around Tsuruhashi and eat in the Korean market there. Added to the list.

## Book Club verdict
Group average 4.6, our highest of the year. Everyone cried at a different place.
