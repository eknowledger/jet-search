# The Overstory

**Richard Powers** · finished May 2026 · ★★★★ · [[Book Club]] June pick (mine — sorry, everyone)

Nine people, each with a tree somewhere in their story, slowly drawn together into the fight over the last old-growth forests of the American West. Over 500 pages. I broke the club's page rule for it, and I'd do it again. Mostly.

## What I thought
The first section, "Roots" — the separate stories — is the best thing I read all year. Each one is a whole life in thirty pages: the chestnut photographed once a month for decades, the mulberry in a Chinese-American family's garden, the botanist who discovers that trees signal to each other and is laughed out of her field for it. I read those slowly.

The middle, the activism, sags. The characters turn into positions. Then the last part comes back to time — tree time — and it got me.

## Quotes
> "The best arguments in the world won't change a person's mind. The only thing that can do that is a good story."

> "This is not our world with trees in it. It's a world of trees, where humans have just arrived."

The first one is more or less why I'm writing [[The Lighthouse Keeper]].

## What it changed
- I look at Lisbon's jacarandas differently. In May the whole city turns purple, and now I keep wondering how old they are and who planted them.
- On the terrace I've started treating the [[Lemon tree]] less like a decoration and more like a resident. It was there, on its own terms, before the tomatoes.
- I'd read [[Braiding Sweetgrass]] just before, which was the right order: Kimmerer's gratitude, then Powers's grief.

## Book Club verdict
Group average 3.9. Duarte loved the science — the trees talking to each other through the fungi underground. Tiago didn't finish and said it was too heavy to carry on the ferry. Literally.
