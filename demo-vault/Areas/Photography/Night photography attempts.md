# Night photography attempts

Night is the hardest thing for me. Notes on what I tried, what failed, and what to try next.

## Attempt 1 — Tram 28 light trails, Portas do Sol (2026-05-23)

- X-T5, 23 mm, camera resting on the wall, no tripod: f/8, 4 s, ISO 125.
- 31 frames, 2 usable. The wall isn't level, so everything tilts. Trams at night are fewer than I thought: one every 12–15 minutes.
- Lesson: bring a tripod, or at least a small beanbag.

## Attempt 2 — Cinestill 800T, handheld (roll 6, May–June 2026)

- OM-1 at 1/30, f/1.8, metered on the street lamps.
- Halation around every lamp, a lovely red glow, but most frames came out two stops underexposed. Four keepers out of 36 ([[Film rolls log]]).
- Lesson: meter for the shadows, not the lamps. Or push the film one stop.

## Attempt 3 — Castelo de São Jorge from Senhora do Monte, blue hour (2026-08-29)

- Tripod borrowed from Sofia. X-T5 at f/8, 10 s, ISO 125, 2-second timer. Tried both Tram 28 Mono and Lisbon Gold ([[Film simulation recipes]]).
- Blue hour lasted about 20 minutes, and I spent ten of them setting up.
- Three good frames. The best: the castle lit up, the sky deep blue, the city lights just coming on. A candidate for the night chapter of the [[Photo book for Sofia]].
- Lesson: set up before sunset, compose, then wait.

## Attempt 4 — Handheld, high ISO, Alfama alleys (2026-10-10)

- X-T5, f/2, 1/30, ISO 6400, Lisbon Gold. The grain is fine; the real problem is camera shake.
- The in-body stabilisation helps: at 1/8, about one frame in three is sharp, as long as nobody is moving.
- 54 frames, 9 keepers. The best night so far.
- Lessons: lean against a wall, breathe out, shoot a burst of three. The middle frame is usually the sharpest.

## What I know now

| Situation | Settings that did the trick |
|---|---|
| Blue hour, tripod | f/8, 5–15 s, ISO 125, 2 s timer |
| Street lamps, handheld | f/2, 1/30–1/60, ISO 3200–6400 |
| Light trails | f/11, 10–20 s, ISO 125, tripod |
| Film at night | Cinestill 800T, or Portra 400 pushed one stop; meter for the shadows |
