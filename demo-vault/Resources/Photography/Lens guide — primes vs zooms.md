# Lens guide — primes vs zooms

A **prime** has one focal length. A **zoom** covers a range. Neither is better; they make you shoot differently.

## The short version

| | Primes | Zooms |
|---|---|---|
| Size and weight | small, light | bigger, heavier |
| Maximum aperture | wide (f/1.4–f/2) | narrower (f/2.8–f/4, or variable) |
| Low light | better | needs higher ISO or stabilisation |
| Background blur | more | less |
| Flexibility | you move your feet | you turn a ring |
| Cost | cheap to expensive | middle to very expensive |
| Effect on how you see | trains one way of looking | lets you change your mind |

## Focal length and crop

On an APS-C camera like the X-T5, multiply by **1.5** to get the full-frame equivalent field of view.

| Fujifilm lens | Equivalent | Character | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|
| XF 16 mm f/2.8 | 24 mm | wide: streets, interiors, landscapes | 155 g |
| **XF 23 mm f/2** | **35 mm** | the classic street focal length: wide enough for context, close enough for people | 180 g |
| XF 33 mm f/1.4 | 50 mm | "normal": the way the eye sees | 360 g |
| XF 50 mm f/2 | 75 mm | short tele: portraits, details | 200 g |
| XF 18-55 mm f/2.8-4 | 27–84 mm | the kit zoom, sharper than its reputation | 310 g |
| XF 16-80 mm f/4 | 24–122 mm | the travel zoom | 440 g |

On the film OM-1 there is no crop: a 50 mm is a 50 mm.

## When a prime makes sense

- **Street photography.** Knowing the frame before you raise the camera is half the battle. After a few months with the 23 mm, I see in 35 mm.
- **Low light**, indoors and at night: f/2 is a stop faster than f/2.8 and two stops faster than f/4.
- **Travel light.** The X-T5 with the 23 mm weighs about 740 g and fits in a small bag.
- **Learning composition.** One focal length takes away a decision and makes you move ([[Composition — rule of thirds and beyond]]).

## When a zoom makes sense

- **Travel where you can't move:** a boat, a crowd, a viewpoint behind a fence.
- **Events**, where you can't ask people to wait while you change lenses.
- **Landscapes and wildlife**, where the distance is fixed and you aren't.
- **One-lens trips** where the subjects vary: temples, food, mountains, people.

## "Zoom with your feet" (and its limits)

Walking closer changes the framing, but not the perspective. A 75 mm from far away compresses the background; a 35 mm up close stretches it. Feet and zoom ring are not the same thing. That said, for street photography, closer is almost always better.

## Questions to ask before buying a lens

1. What photo did I miss twice because I didn't have it?
2. Would I carry it every time, or only on "special" walks?
3. Can I rent it for a weekend first?
