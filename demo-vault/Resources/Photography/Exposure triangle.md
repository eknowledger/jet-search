# Exposure triangle

Exposure is how much light reaches the sensor or the film. Three settings control it, and each one also changes how the picture looks. The second part is what matters.

## The three sides

| Setting | Controls light by | Side effect | A bigger number means |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aperture** (f-number) | the size of the opening in the lens | depth of field | a smaller opening: less light, more in focus |
| **Shutter speed** | how long the shutter stays open | motion blur or frozen movement | (as a fraction, 1/500 vs 1/60) faster: less light, less blur |
| **ISO** | sensitivity, or amplification | noise on digital, grain on film | brighter, noisier |

## Stops

One **stop** is double or half the light. Everything in exposure is counted in stops.

- **Aperture:** f/1.4 → f/2 → f/2.8 → f/4 → f/5.6 → f/8 → f/11 → f/16. Each step lets in half the light.
- **Shutter:** 1/1000 → 1/500 → 1/250 → 1/125 → 1/60 → 1/30 → 1/15.
- **ISO:** 100 → 200 → 400 → 800 → 1600 → 3200 → 6400.

The trick is trading between them. f/4 at 1/250 and ISO 400 gives the same brightness as f/5.6 at 1/125 and ISO 400, or f/5.6 at 1/250 and ISO 800. Same brightness, three different pictures.

## Which side to protect

- **A portrait against a busy street:** aperture first. Open up (f/2) for a soft background.
- **Street, people walking:** shutter first. 1/250 freezes walking; 1/500 for cyclists and children.
- **Landscape, everything sharp:** f/8 to f/11, and a tripod if the shutter gets slow.
- **Night, handheld:** the slowest shutter you can hold, the widest aperture, and whatever ISO it takes. A noisy sharp photo beats a clean blurry one.

## Rules of thumb

- **Sunny 16:** on a bright sunny day, f/16 with a shutter speed of 1/ISO. With Portra 400: f/16 at 1/500 (close enough to 1/400). Then trade: f/8 at 1/2000.
- **Handheld limit:** the slowest safe speed is about 1 / (focal length × crop factor). A 23 mm lens on APS-C → 1/35, so 1/30 with care. In-body stabilisation buys three to five stops for still subjects, but not for moving people.
- **Film latitude:** colour negative film like Portra 400 forgives overexposure (two or three stops) much better than underexposure. When in doubt, overexpose film. Digital is the opposite: protect the highlights, because blown highlights are gone for good.

## Exposure compensation

In aperture or shutter priority the camera decides the rest, and it gets fooled. White walls in sunlight make it underexpose; dark alleys make it overexpose. In Alfama I use about −2/3 for sunlit white walls and +2/3 for faces in the shade. The histogram, or the highlight warning in the viewfinder, is more honest than the screen.

## An example from a walk

Golden hour on the Panteão, October: aperture priority, f/4, Auto ISO settled at 200, and the camera chose 1/500. The wall was bright and the street dark, so I dialled −1/3 to keep the colour in the stone. On film the same scene got f/4 at 1/500 with Portra at box speed, metered for the street, not the wall.
