# Developing C-41 at home

Colour negative films (Portra, Kodak Gold, Cinestill) use the C-41 process. It sounds scary because of the temperature, 39 °C held within a few tenths of a degree for the developer, but with a sous-vide stick it's easy. I've developed five rolls at home since August 2026; the cost per roll went from about €12 at the lab to about €3.

## What you need

- **A C-41 kit.** I use the Cinestill Cs41 liquid kit: a developer and a blix (bleach and fix in one), plus a separate bottle of stabiliser.
- **A developing tank and reels.** Paterson, with two reels.
- **A changing bag**, or a truly dark bathroom at night.
- **A sous-vide stick** in a plastic tub as the water bath.
- **A second thermometer.** Don't trust the sous-vide display alone.
- **Three brown 1 L bottles**, labelled.
- Measuring jugs, a funnel, gloves, a timer, clips for hanging.
- **Distilled water** for mixing and for the stabiliser.

## Step by step

**Once:** mix the kit following its instructions, with distilled water. Label the bottles with the date and keep a tally of rolls on each one.

1. **Load the film** onto the reel inside the changing bag. Practise first with a dead roll in daylight: this is the hardest part.
2. **Warm everything.** Put the developer and blix bottles in the 39 °C bath for about 30 minutes, and the empty tank too.
3. **Pre-wash.** Fill the tank with water at 39 °C, agitate gently for 1 minute, pour out. The water comes out coloured; that's normal.
4. **Developer: 3:30 at 39 °C.** Pour in and start the timer. Agitate continuously for the first 10 seconds, then 4 gentle inversions every 30 seconds. Tap the tank on the counter to release bubbles. Pour the developer back into its bottle; it's reusable.
5. **Blix: 8:00.** Anywhere between 35 and 40 °C is fine. Same agitation. Pour back into its bottle.
6. **Wash.** 3 minutes in running water at about 38 °C, or 10 fill-and-empty cycles with a few inversions each.
7. **Stabiliser: 1 minute**, no agitation. Don't rinse afterwards.
8. **Hang to dry** in the bathroom for 2–3 hours, with a clip at the bottom as a weight. Run the hot shower for a minute first to settle the dust.
9. **Cut and sleeve** in strips of six frames.

The developer gets weaker with each roll, so the developing time goes up. The kit's chart tells you by how much; write every roll on the bottle so you know where you are.

## Problems I've had

| Problem | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Dense negatives, colours off (roll 9) | The bath dropped to 37 °C during developing | Lid on the tub, check with the second thermometer |
| Uneven bands near the sprocket holes | Too much agitation | Gentle inversions, no shaking |
| Water spots | Tap water in the last rinse | Distilled water and stabiliser |
| A kinked frame | Film slipped on the reel | Cut the corners of the film, load slowly |

## Safety and disposal

- Wear gloves and keep the room ventilated. The blix smells and irritates skin.
- **Never pour used blix down the drain**: it contains silver. Used chemistry goes in a sealed bottle to the ecocentro, the municipal collection point for hazardous waste.
- Keep chemistry away from food, and keep the cat out of the bathroom.
