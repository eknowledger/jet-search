# Golden hour and blue hour

The two times of day when the light does the heavy lifting for you.

## What they are

- **Golden hour:** roughly when the sun is between 6° above the horizon and 4° below it. Low, warm, directional light; long shadows; skin looks good; walls glow. Despite the name, it's often much shorter than an hour.
- **Blue hour:** after sunset (or before sunrise), when the sun is about 4° to 8° below the horizon. No direct sun, a deep blue sky, and city lights that start to balance the sky. Short: around 20 minutes in Lisbon.

How long they last depends on the latitude and the season. The further from the equator, and the closer to midsummer, the slower the sun sinks, and the longer the good light lasts.

## Lisbon, approximate times

| Date | Sunrise | Sunset | Evening golden hour | Evening blue hour |

| 21 June | 06:12 | 21:05 | 20:25–21:25 | 21:25–21:50 |
| 17 October | 07:48 | 18:56 | 18:20–19:15 | 19:15–19:35 |
| 26 October (after the clocks go back) | 06:57 | 17:45 | 17:10–18:05 | 18:05–18:25 |
| 21 December | 07:51 | 17:17 | 16:40–17:35 | 17:35–18:00 |

In the morning it's the mirror image: blue hour first, then golden hour after sunrise. For exact times on a given day, use a planning app such as PhotoPills, or any sunrise table.

For the [[Japan Trip 2027]] (early April): Tokyo sunrise around 05:25 and sunset around 18:00; Kyoto about 05:45 and 18:20. Fushimi Inari at 6 am means arriving in the morning golden hour, which is exactly the point.

## How to shoot golden hour

- **Don't let auto white balance eat the warmth.** Use a fixed white balance: 5200 K, as in Lisbon Gold ([[Film simulation recipes]]), or "daylight".
- **Expose for the highlights** on digital; the shadows will be deep, and that's fine. On film, expose for the shadows ([[Exposure triangle]]).
- **Side light** shows texture: tiles, stone, wrinkles. **Backlight** gives rim light and flare; shade the lens with your hand if the flare gets too much.
- **Move fast.** In October the light on a given wall lasts 15–20 minutes.

## How to shoot blue hour

- The best moment is often 20–30 minutes after sunset, when the sky and the lit buildings are about equally bright.
- **Tripod**, base ISO, f/8, a few seconds of exposure, 2-second timer.
- Handheld is possible with a wide aperture and high ISO, but the sky goes noisy fast.
- **White balance:** 5200 K keeps the sky blue and the street lamps orange. Auto WB tends to neutralise both, and the photo goes grey.
- Set up before sunset. Blue hour is too short to spend it walking around looking for a spot ([[Night photography attempts]]).

## Lisbon notes

- Alfama faces the river, so in the morning the sun comes up over the Tejo and lights the façades from the side.
- Evening: the Panteão and São Vicente catch the last light from the west; the Miradouro da Senhora do Monte faces the castle and the sunset.
- After the clocks go back in late October, golden hour comes an hour earlier on the clock: easier to catch on a weekday.
- Cloudy days can be the best: if a gap opens in the west just before sunset, the light under the clouds is spectacular.
