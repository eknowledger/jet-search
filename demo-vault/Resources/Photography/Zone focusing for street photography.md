# Zone focusing for street photography

Autofocus is fast, but not as fast as not focusing at all. With zone focusing you set the focus distance in advance and choose an aperture deep enough that everything in a "zone" is sharp. Then you just raise the camera and press.

## Why bother

- No hunting for focus in low light or against busy backgrounds.
- You can shoot without looking through the viewfinder.
- People react less when you aren't standing there focusing on them.
- It is the only practical way on a manual-focus film camera like the OM-1.

## How on the X-T5 with the XF 23 mm f/2

1. Set the focus mode selector to **M** (manual).
2. In the menu, set the **depth-of-field scale** to **film format basis**. The "pixel basis" option is much stricter and shows a tiny zone.
3. Choose the aperture: **f/8** in daylight, **f/5.6** in shade.
4. Turn the focus ring while watching the distance bar in the viewfinder until the focus point sits at **3 m**. The white part of the bar is the zone.
5. Set **Auto ISO** with a minimum shutter speed of **1/500**, so movement is frozen.
6. Optional: assign AF-ON to a button, so you can still autofocus one shot when you need it.

The 23 mm f/2 has no distance scale on the barrel, so the viewfinder bar is the only guide. Check it now and then: the ring is easy to bump.

## The numbers

APS-C, 23 mm, circle of confusion 0.02 mm. Approximate.

| Aperture | Focus at | Sharp from | to |
|---|---|---|---|
| f/8 | 2 m | 1.25 m | 5 m |
| f/8 | 3 m | 1.6 m | 30 m |
| f/5.6 | 2.5 m | 1.6 m | 5.3 m |
| f/11 | 2.5 m | 1.2 m | infinity |

The hyperfocal distance at f/8 is about 3.3 m: focus there, and everything from about 1.7 m to infinity is acceptably sharp.

In Alfama's alleys I use **2 m at f/8**; on the wider squares, 3–4 m.

## On film: the OM-1

The Zuiko 50 mm f/1.8 has a depth-of-field scale engraved on the lens. At f/8, focused at 3 m, the zone is only about 2.3 m to 4.2 m: much thinner than on the 23 mm, because the lens is longer. A 28 mm is far better for this: at f/8 focused at 2.5 m, roughly 1.4 m to 10 m. The Zuiko 28 mm f/2.8 is on the [[Gear wishlist]].

## Practice drill

1. Pick a spot about 3 m from where people walk: a doorway, a bench, the edge of a staircase.
2. Shoot 50 frames without looking through the viewfinder.
3. At home, count how many are sharp and how many are well framed. Framing is the hard part; sharpness takes care of itself.
4. Repeat next week at 2 m.

## Etiquette

Zone focusing makes candid photos easy. That doesn't make every candid photo fair. Don't photograph people in distress, smile if someone notices you, and delete if you're asked to. If someone asks for a print, send one.
