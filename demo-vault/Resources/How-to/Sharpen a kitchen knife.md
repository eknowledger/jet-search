# Sharpen a kitchen knife

With a whetstone. A sharp knife is safer than a blunt one — it goes where you aim it instead of skidding off a tomato. Our knives: a Victorinox chef's knife, a small paring knife and a bread knife (serrated — leave that one alone).

## You need

- A combination whetstone, **1000 / 6000** grit (1000 sharpens, 6000 polishes)
- A damp tea towel under the stone so it doesn't slide
- A bowl of water
- A marker pen (optional, but it helps)

## Angle

- European knives (like the Victorinox): about **20°**
- Japanese knives: about **15°**

On my 4.5 cm blade, 15° means the spine sits a bit over 1 cm off the stone; 20°, about 1.5 cm. Colour the edge with the marker — after a few strokes you can see whether you're grinding the very edge (good) or just the shoulder above it (angle too low).

## Steps

1. **Soak the stone** 10–15 minutes until it stops bubbling (soaking stones only — splash-and-go ones just need a wet surface).
2. **1000 side up**, on the towel, short end towards you.
3. **Hold the angle.** Fingers of the other hand on the flat of the blade, near the edge.
4. **Push the edge across the stone** as if slicing a thin layer off it, heel to tip in one sweeping motion. Light pressure on the push, almost none on the pull back.
5. **About 10–15 strokes**, then feel the *other* side of the edge with your thumb — across the edge, never along it. You're looking for a **burr**: a tiny rough lip along the whole length.
6. **Burr along the whole edge?** Turn the knife and do the same on the other side until the burr flips back.
7. **Turn the stone to 6000.** Fewer strokes, lighter, alternating sides: 5 and 5, then 3 and 3, then 1 and 1. This removes the burr and polishes the edge.
8. **Strop** — a few edge-trailing strokes on folded newspaper or the back of an old belt.
9. **Rinse and dry** the knife. Let the stone dry on its side before putting it away.

## Testing

- Slices a sheet of paper cleanly, no tearing.
- Bites into a tomato skin with no pressure.
- Shaves a thin curl off a sheet of paper's edge — show-off level.

## Honing between sharpenings

The rod in the knife block doesn't sharpen; it straightens an edge that has rolled over. A few light strokes before cooking, same angle. Hard Japanese knives: skip it or use a ceramic rod, gently.

## Looking after the stone

Stones wear hollow in the middle. Every few sessions, rub it flat on a flattening plate or on wet sandpaper on a sheet of glass.
