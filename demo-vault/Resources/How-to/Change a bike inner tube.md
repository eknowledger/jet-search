# Change a bike inner tube

For the old road bike I ride along the river to Belém. Tyres are **700 × 28c**, tubes have **Presta** valves (the thin ones with the little nut on top). Takes me about 15 minutes at home, 25 at the side of the road with Rui offering advice.

## Kit in the saddle bag

- Spare tube, 700 × 25–32c, Presta, 48 mm valve
- Two plastic tyre levers
- Mini pump (Presta head) — or a CO₂ cartridge as backup
- Multitool
- Patch kit for the old tube later
- Disposable gloves (the chain)

## Steps

1. **Shift to the smallest rear cog** if it's the back wheel — makes it easier to get the wheel back in.
2. **Open the brake** (rim brakes: the little lever on the caliper) so the tyre fits past the pads.
3. **Take the wheel out.** Quick release lever open, unscrew a few turns, drop the wheel out.
4. **Let all the air out.** Unscrew the Presta tip and press it. Unscrew the valve's lock ring at the rim.
5. **Lever one side of the tyre off.** Start opposite the valve. Hook one lever under the bead and onto a spoke, a second lever 10 cm along, then slide it round.
6. **Pull the tube out**, valve last.
7. **Find out why it went flat.** Pump up the old tube a bit and listen — the hole tells you where to look on the tyre. Run your fingers *carefully* round the inside of the tyre for glass or thorns. Check the rim tape covers every spoke hole. If you skip this, the new tube goes flat in five minutes.
8. **Give the new tube a little air** so it holds its shape and doesn't twist.
9. **Valve in first**, through the rim hole, then tuck the tube into the tyre all the way round.
10. **Push the bead back on with your thumbs**, starting at the valve and going both ways. The last bit opposite the valve is tight — push the bead already on into the centre channel of the rim to gain a few millimetres. Avoid levers here if you can: they pinch the tube.
11. **Check nothing is caught.** Go round squeezing the tyre and look between tyre and rim. No tube should be visible.
12. **Pump up slowly**, checking the line on the tyre sits evenly above the rim on both sides.
13. **Pressure:** for 28 mm tyres I use about **5.5 bar front, 6 bar rear** (80–90 psi).
14. Wheel back in, close the quick release firmly, **close the brake again**, spin the wheel.

## Common mistakes

- Forgetting to close the brake. Did it once on the hill down to Santa Apolónia. Never again.
- A pinch flat straight away — tube caught under the bead in step 10.
- Valve going in crooked — straighten it before full pressure.

## Patching the old one

At home: find the hole (bowl of water, bubbles), roughen the area, glue, wait until it's dry to the touch, patch, press hard for a minute. Patched tubes become the spares.
