# Descale the coffee machine

For the De'Longhi Dedica (see [[Appliances]]). Lisbon tap water is moderately hard, so limescale builds up in the boiler and pipes — the coffee gets cooler, slower and more bitter. Every **two months**, or when the descale light comes on, whichever is first. About 45 minutes, most of it waiting.

## You need

- Descaler made for espresso machines (De'Longhi's own, or any citric/lactic acid one). **Not vinegar** — it's hard on the seals and the manufacturer says no.
- A jug or bowl that holds at least 1.5 L
- A cloth

## Steps

1. **Empty the drip tray** and take out the water filter if one is fitted.
2. **Fill the tank** with descaler and water in the ratio on the bottle (for mine: 100 ml descaler, then water up to the 1 L line).
3. **Put the jug under** both the coffee spout and the steam wand.
4. **Start the descale programme.** On my model it's in the manual under "Descaling" (holding a button combination until the light flashes). It runs by itself in bursts, with pauses for the solution to soak.
5. When the tank is empty, **rinse the tank well** and fill it with clean water to MAX.
6. **Run the rinse** — the programme continues once the tank is back in.
7. **Empty the jug** and run one more full tank of clean water through the spout and the wand, to be sure.
8. Put the filter back, wipe everything, make a test coffee and throw it away.

## If there's no proper descaler

Citric acid from the supermarket baking aisle: one tablespoon (about 15 g) in 500 ml of warm water. Then two full rinses instead of one.

## Between descales

- Purge the steam wand after every milk, wipe it straight away.
- Empty the drip tray every day — Pimenta likes to drink from it.
- Once a month, rinse the portafilter basket in hot water with a drop of soap.
