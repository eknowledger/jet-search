# The Pomodoro technique

*Summary of Francesco Cirillo's own book,* The Pomodoro Technique, *and a few blog posts by people who adapted it. I use it mostly for admin and revision, sometimes for the novel.*

## Where it comes from

Francesco Cirillo came up with it as a university student in Italy in the late 1980s. He was struggling to concentrate, took a tomato-shaped kitchen timer — *pomodoro* is Italian for tomato — and challenged himself to study for just ten minutes without stopping. The ten minutes grew into the classic 25.

## The method

1. **Pick one task.**
2. **Set a timer for 25 minutes.** That block is one *pomodoro*.
3. **Do only that task** until the timer rings.
4. **Mark an X** on a sheet of paper.
5. **Short break: 5 minutes.** Get up. Water. Not the phone, ideally.
6. **After four pomodoros, a longer break** — 15 to 30 minutes.

The rules Cirillo is strict about:

- A pomodoro is **indivisible**. If you stop halfway, it doesn't count; you start again.
- If you finish early, use the rest to review or improve what you did.
- **Interruptions** get handled with "inform, negotiate, call back": tell the person you'll get back to them, agree when, and do it. Internal interruptions ("I should check the boiler booking!") get written down with a small mark on the sheet and dealt with later.

## Why it helps

- It makes big, vague tasks **small and countable**. "Do the tax return" becomes "four pomodoros".
- A visible end makes it easier to start — the key point in [[Why we procrastinate]].
- The breaks are built in, so there's no guilt in taking them.
- Counting X's at the end of the day is oddly satisfying. Good data, too: I now know the tax return really takes four.

## Where it doesn't fit

- **Deep writing.** 25 minutes is often just when a scene starts flowing. Stopping then is painful.
- Tasks that come in tiny pieces (answering messages) — better batched.
- Days with lots of fixed appointments.

## How I've adapted it

| Task | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Admin, bills, forms | 25 / 5 | classic; [[Tax return 2025]] took four |
| Novel — first draft | 50 / 10 | two of these = the 600 words before breakfast, most days |
| Novel — Sunday revision | 25 / 5 | revision is tiring, short blocks help |
| Spanish and Japanese | 25 / 5 | then 5 minutes of reviewing out loud |
| Renovation research | 25 / 5 | stops me falling into tile catalogues for two hours |

- The timer is a red plastic tomato from a kitchen shop in Porto. A phone timer would mean a phone on the desk.
- Breaks on the terrace when it's not raining — check the tomatoes, come back.
- If I can't even start, I allow myself **one** pomodoro and permission to stop after it. I rarely stop.
