# Urban beekeeping

*Summary of what I read when Rui suggested a hive for the terrace — a handful of beekeeping guides, a piece on the bees on the roof of the Paris opera house, and research from London on whether cities have too many hives. Short answer for us: not yet, maybe never.*

## Why people keep bees in cities

- Cities can be surprisingly good for bees: parks, balcony flowers, street trees, fewer pesticides than intensive farmland.
- Paris has hives on the Palais Garnier and had them on Notre-Dame — they survived the 2019 fire.
- City honey is often varied and good because the flowers are so mixed.
- It's a way to feel closer to the seasons. Same reason as the [[Rooftop Garden]], really.

## What a colony needs

- **Space and a stable spot.** A colony can be 20,000–60,000 bees in summer. A full hive is heavy.
- **Shelter from the wind.** Bees struggle to fly in strong wind, and a hive can blow over on a roof.
- **Water nearby**, every day, especially in summer — otherwise they'll use the neighbours' pool or dripping air conditioners.
- **Forage within a few kilometres.** Bees usually fly up to about 3 km, further when they must.
- **A beekeeper who checks** every week or two in spring and summer.

## Hive types

| Type | Good | Less good |
|---|---|---|
| Langstroth | standard frames, easy to buy parts | heavy boxes to lift |
| Top-bar | no heavy lifting, natural comb | less honey, combs fragile |
| Warré | low intervention, vertical | harder to inspect |

## The problems

- **Varroa mites** — the biggest threat to honeybees everywhere. Hives need monitoring and treatment, or they collapse.
- **Swarming.** In spring a strong colony splits and half flies off with the old queen. In a city that means a cloud of bees on a lamp post and a call to the fire brigade.
- **Neighbours.** Bees use the same flight path; someone's laundry will be in it. Allergies are real.
- **Too many hives.** In London and Paris, research has warned that hive numbers grew faster than the flowers. Honeybees can then outcompete the **wild bees** — and there are hundreds of wild bee species that pollinate far better than honeybees for some plants.

## The rules here

In Portugal every apiary has to be registered with the DGAV, even a single hive, and there are distance rules from houses and roads. A shared building probably also needs the neighbours' agreement.

## For our terrace

The terrace is 18 m², south-west facing, on the 6th floor and **windy**. Honestly: a bad place for a hive. No water source except the tap, the neighbours' washing lines right next to it, and Pimenta.

What we're doing instead:

- [ ] A **bee hotel** for solitary bees (mason bees) on the sheltered east wall — bamboo tubes, 6–8 mm
- [ ] More flowers for pollinators in the pots: borage (great next to tomatoes), lavender, rosemary, oregano left to flower
- [ ] A shallow water dish with stones so bees can land
- [ ] Talk to the beekeeping association about a course in spring, just to learn
