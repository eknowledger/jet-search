# Speech — final draft

Final version, 2025-09-01. Read aloud at a calm pace: **4:05**, so about 4 minutes. The words in *italics* are stage directions to myself. Copied onto six numbered cue cards. For how it got here see [[Feedback from Rui]].

---

Boa noite a todos — and good evening to everyone who came all the way from Cork.

For those who don't know me, I'm Noa, Sofia's big sister. I've known Sofia for thirty years, which means I have a lot of material. She has made me promise, in writing, to use almost none of it.

So I'm going to tell you one story. It's about a piece of paper.

*(pause)*

Seven years ago, in the summer of 2018, Sofia and I went to the Azores. Two sisters, one rucksack each, and a plan that Sofia had made — which means a plan written on the back of a receipt. On our third day we were in Horta, on Faial, waiting for the morning ferry to Pico. We had bought the tickets the night before. And Sofia was in charge of the tickets.

I'd like that on the record. Sofia was in charge of the tickets.

*(wait for the laugh)*

The queue starts moving. I hand over mine. Sofia puts her hand in her pocket. Then the other pocket. Then her rucksack. Then *my* rucksack, for some reason. And then she gives me the face she has made since she was four — the face that says, "this is somehow your fault."

The ferry was full. The next one was three hours later. And the man standing behind us in the queue — tall, sunburnt, holding a terrible paperback — said, "Take mine. I'm not in a hurry." Before she could argue, he had put his ticket in her hand and walked off to find a coffee.

That was Bernardo.

*(look at Bernardo)*

Sofia spent the whole crossing saying she had to find him and pay him back. I said, "Sofia, it's a small island. He'll find you." He did. Three hours later he walked into the only café open in Madalena, sat down at our table, and said, "So. Did you find it?"

She hadn't. Not that day. Three days later, back home in Porto, she opened her book, and there it was. Her ferry ticket. Being used as a bookmark. Sofia — who in thirty years has never used a bookmark. Who folds down the corners of library books, which, Bernardo, you should know, is a crime in our family.

*(slower)*

I've thought about that ticket a lot. Because the truth is, Sofia lost a ticket and found something much better. Bernardo, you were kind to her from the very first minute, before you even knew her name. And in the seven years since, I've watched you keep doing it — making room, waiting for the next boat, turning up at the café at exactly the right time.

Sofia, you have always been the brave one. You said yes first, you tried things first, you jumped off the rocks at Pico first while I was still checking how deep the water was. You're the person I call when something good happens, before anyone else. I am so proud of you. And I'm so happy you found someone who is not in a hurry.

*(look at Bernardo's grandmother)*

The ticket, by the way, is on table four, in a frame. Please don't use it as a bookmark.

*(glass up)*

So, if you'd all raise your glasses. To Sofia and Bernardo — may you always have one spare ticket, and someone happy to wait for the next boat.

Aos noivos! To Sofia and Bernardo!
