# Tomatoes — San Marzano and Cherokee Purple

Two varieties, two purposes. **San Marzano** for sauce — long, meaty, few seeds, and they cook down into the passata I put away for winter. **Cherokee Purple** for eating raw: big, lumpy, dusky red-purple with green shoulders, cut thick with salt, olive oil and nothing else.

## 2026 timeline
- **Sown indoors on 2026-02-15** — 12 cells of San Marzano, 8 of Cherokee Purple, on a heat mat at 24 °C. San Marzano came up in 7 days, Cherokee Purple in 10.
- Potted on into 9 cm pots in mid-March; hardening off from 12 April (out on the terrace by day, in at night).
- **Planted out on 2026-04-20** — six San Marzano and four Cherokee Purple in the two long troughs by the parapet, 45 cm apart, buried deep up to the first leaves. Two canes each because of the wind.
- **First harvest on 2026-07-08** — a single Cherokee Purple, 310 g. [[Rui]] and I ate it standing up on the terrace.
- Pulled the plants on 4 October.

## How it went

| | San Marzano | Cherokee Purple |
|---|---|---|
| Plants | 6 | 4 |
| Harvest | about 9 kg | about 5 kg |
| Problems | blossom end rot in July | cracking after the September rain |
| Verdict | grow again | grow again, but only 2 plants |

**Blossom end rot** showed up on the San Marzano in mid-July: black, leathery bottoms on the first trusses. It's a calcium problem on paper, but really a watering problem — the plant can't move calcium into the fruit when the soil swings between soaked and dry. I'd been topping up by hand on top of the drip. Once the [[Drip irrigation]] was left alone on its steady 12 minutes every morning, the new fruit came clean, and the doubled watering kept everything alive through the August heat waves.

**Cracking**: Cherokee Purple split badly after the first heavy rain in September. Next time, pick a day early when rain is forecast and let them finish ripening on the windowsill.

## Passata day (6 September)
7 kg of San Marzano → 10 jars of 500 ml. The method is in [[Cooking]]. Last year's jars ran out in February; this year's should get us to April.
