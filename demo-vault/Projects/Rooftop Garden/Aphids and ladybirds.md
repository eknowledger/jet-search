# Aphids and ladybirds

## What happened
Early June: the new leaves on the [[Chillies]] curled inwards and went sticky. Underneath, packed along the veins, were green aphids — hundreds of them — and a line of ants marching up the stems to farm them.

What I tried first:
- A hard spray of water every morning. Knocked them off; they came back.
- Diluted soft potassium soap. Killed some, scorched a few leaves in the afternoon sun.
- Squashing by hand. Satisfying, pointless.

Then I ordered **ladybird larvae**: a tube of **50** from a biological pest-control supplier. They arrived by post in wood shavings, looking like tiny grey alligators.

## Release — 2026-06-14
- At dusk, when it's cool and they're less likely to wander off.
- Misted the chilli leaves first so they had something to drink.
- Brushed them straight onto the worst leaves, and a few onto the lemon tree.
- No soap spray afterwards — it kills them too.

## Result
The aphids were **gone in 2 weeks**. By 28 June I couldn't find a single colony. The larvae ate their way through everything and then pupated, several of them on the [[Lemon tree]], and in July there were adult ladybirds all over the terrace. The ants gave up.

Cost: €24 for the tube. Cheaper than anything in a spray bottle, and nothing to wash off the chillies.

## For next year
- Order the larvae at the end of May, before the outbreak.
- Sow nasturtiums, calendula and sweet alyssum near the chillies — they bring in hoverflies, whose larvae also eat aphids.
- Go easy on nitrogen feed for the chillies: soft, fast growth is exactly what aphids like.
- Look under the leaves every week from May. This year it took me a week to notice.
