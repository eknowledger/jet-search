# Chillies

Three 20-litre pots against the warmest wall. Chillies love the roof: full sun, heat bouncing off the tiles, and once they're staked they don't mind the wind.

## 2026 varieties

| Variety | Heat | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Padrón | mostly mild, one in ten is hot | Best plant of the year. Fried in olive oil with flaky salt, eaten with fingers. |
| Malagueta (piri-piri) | hot | Small, upright pods. Makes the piri-piri oil. |
| Jalapeño | medium | Fine. Not exciting. Won't grow it again. |
| Aji Amarillo | medium-hot | Huge plant, flowered in August, never ripened. The season is too short even here. |

Sown indoors with the tomatoes on 15 February, out at the end of April.

## Aphids
In June the aphids found the chillies — the soft new growth at the tips. Fifty ladybird larvae on 14 June cleared them in two weeks. The whole story: [[Aphids and ladybirds]].

## Piri-piri oil
Malagueta pods, a garlic clove, a bay leaf, a strip of peel from the [[Lemon tree]], a splash of aguardente, topped up with olive oil. A month in a dark cupboard. Written up in [[Cooking]].

## Overwintering
Chillies are perennials that everyone treats as annuals. This year I'll try to keep the malagueta: cut it back by two-thirds in November, fleece it on cold nights alongside the lemon tree, water less. If it survives, it starts next season two months ahead.
