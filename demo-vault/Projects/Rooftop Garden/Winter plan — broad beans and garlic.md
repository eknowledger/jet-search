# Winter plan — broad beans and garlic

First year growing anything through the winter. The troughs would otherwise sit empty from October to April, and **broad beans and garlic go in in October** and are both happy with a mild, wet Lisbon winter. Plus **fleece for the lemon tree**.

## Broad beans
- Variety: 'Aquadulce Claudia' — short, hardy, bred for autumn sowing.
- Two rows in the big trough, 15 cm apart, 5 cm deep.
- Netting on canes along the rows: they're short, but the wind up here would flatten them.
- Pinch out the tips when the first pods set (and eat the tips, fried with garlic).
- Harvest April to May. Then leave the roots in the soil — beans fix nitrogen, and the tomatoes follow them in the same trough.

## Garlic
- 4 heads of purple garlic from the market, biggest cloves only — 24 cloves.
- Pointy end up, 3 cm deep, 10 cm apart, in the second trough.
- It needs a cold spell to split into cloves. A Lisbon winter on a windy roof should just about do it.
- Harvest in June, when the lower leaves turn yellow. Dry it hanging under the stairs.

## Lemon tree
Fleece on cold or stormy nights — details in [[Lemon tree]].

## Also
- Basil inside when nights drop below 12 °C ([[Herbs — basil, mint, parsley]]).
- Irrigation to every other day from November ([[Drip irrigation]]).
- Try to overwinter the malagueta ([[Chillies]]).
