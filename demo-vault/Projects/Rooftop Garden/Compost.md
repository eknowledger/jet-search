# Compost

A 220-litre bin with a lid, tucked behind the water tank where the wind can't knock it over. Kitchen scraps come up from our flat, and the café downstairs gives me their coffee grounds on Fridays.

## The ratio
**Browns to greens 3:1 by volume.** In practice: every bucket of greens gets three buckets of browns. I keep a crate of torn cardboard next to the bin so there's never an excuse.

| Greens (wet, nitrogen) | Browns (dry, carbon) |
|---|---|
| Fruit and veg peelings | Shredded cardboard |
| Coffee grounds, tea leaves | Egg boxes |
| Plant trimmings, spent tomato plants | Dry plane-tree leaves from the street |
| Pimenta's cat grass, once she's done with it | Plain paper, bakery bags |

Never: meat, fish, dairy, oily food, cat litter, or diseased plants (the rusty mint goes in the bin).

## The wet May
In May the bin went **too wet**: a sour smell, a slimy top, and a cloud of fruit flies every time I lifted the lid. Too many greens — a big spring clear-out of the fridge — plus rain getting in through a crack in the lid. **Fixed with cardboard**: two big bags of it, torn up and forked through, and tape over the crack. Two weeks later it was warm in the middle and smelled like a forest floor again.

## Routine
- Turn it every two weeks with a garden fork. On a roof, the trick is picking a calm day.
- Squeeze test: a handful should feel like a wrung-out sponge. Dripping means add browns; dry and crumbly means add greens or a splash of water.
- In summer it's ready in about four months. I sieved it in September and top-dressed both troughs before the [[Winter plan — broad beans and garlic]].

## Why bother
Carrying bags of compost up six floors of Alfama stairs is argument enough. But [[Braiding Sweetgrass]] made me think of it as giving something back rather than getting rid of rubbish, and that stuck.
