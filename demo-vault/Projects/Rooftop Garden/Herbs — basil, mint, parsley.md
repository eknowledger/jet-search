# Herbs — basil, mint, parsley

The herb shelf by the terrace door. Parsley and mint are easy. Basil is the one that keeps dying.

## Why the basil keeps dying
Two plants died in 2025 and two more this spring before I figured it out. It was **overwatering and cold nights**, together.

- **Overwatering.** The basil sat in a plastic pot on a saucer, *and* it had a drip emitter. The soil never dried out, the saucer held standing water, and the roots rotted. The leaves went limp, I thought it was thirsty, so I watered more. Classic.
- **Cold nights.** Basil hates anything under about 12 °C, and on the roof in April and May the nights are colder than the forecast says: six floors up, the wind off the river takes a few degrees with it. Black-edged leaves the morning after a cold night were the sign I kept missing.

What I do now:
1. The basil lives in a **terracotta pot**. Clay breathes and dries out between waterings; the plastic held water like a cup.
2. No drip emitter on the basil any more, and no saucer.
3. I water **only when the top 2 cm of soil are dry** — finger in up to the first knuckle. In summer that's every day or two; in spring sometimes four days.
4. When the night forecast drops **below 12 °C**, the pot comes **inside**. This October it moved in on the 15th. With the kitchen torn apart for the [[Kitchen Renovation]], it sits on the living-room windowsill, as far from the dust as I can get it.

Since the switch in May: one plant, alive, five months old, enormous. Pesto twice.

## Mint
In **its own pot**, because it spreads — given half a chance its runners would take over the whole trough. One pot of spearmint, one of a Moroccan mint for tea. Cut it hard in July and it comes straight back. It doesn't mind the wind at all.

## Parsley
Flat-leaf, two pots: one sown in March, one in September, so there's always a young plant. Parsley goes to seed in its second year, so the old one gets pulled once it starts to bolt. It gets through the winter here without help.

## Watch list
- Basil seedlings damp off if the trays stay wet. Water from below, and less.
- Mint rust: orange spots under the leaves in a wet autumn. Cut it back and bin the cuttings — not into the [[Compost]].
