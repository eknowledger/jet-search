# Setting — Flores, 1962

## The island
- **Flores** is the westernmost island of the Azores — out on the North American plate, with only little Corvo for company to the north. Next stop west is Newfoundland.
- Green, steep, wet. Waterfalls down the cliffs above Fajã Grande; the basalt columns of the Rocha dos Bordões; crater lakes in the middle. In summer the hydrangeas turn the roadsides blue.
- Two towns — Santa Cruz in the east, Lajes in the south — and small villages in the *fajãs*, the flat strips of land at the foot of the cliffs.

## 1962
- Portugal under Salazar: the Estado Novo, the censor, the political police. Nobody in the village says anything political out loud, and the priest reads the newspaper first.
- The colonial war began in Angola in 1961. Young men are being called up; "Angola" is a word people say quickly.
- Emigration: whole families leaving for America and Canada. After the Capelinhos eruption on Faial in 1957–58, the US opened its doors to Azoreans (the Azorean Refugee Act, 1958). Everyone has a cousin in Massachusetts. Elena wanting *Lisbon* instead is somehow more shocking.
- No airport on Flores yet (it came in the 1970s — check the year). Everything — people, mail, paraffin, news — comes by sea.
- Whaling is still going: open boats, lookouts on the headlands, a rocket when a spout is seen. There's a whale factory in Santa Cruz (check: Boqueirão).
- In Lisbon, building of the bridge over the Tagus starts in November 1962. In September it's still only a photograph in the paper — which is all Elena has of it.

## What I invented
- **Ponta Negra lighthouse**, on the western cliffs. First lit 1924. Two white flashes every ten seconds. Fog horn added in 1931: two blasts every thirty seconds. Ninety-six steps.
- **Fajã das Garças**, the village below, about 200 people. A church, a shop, a landing of black stones, no road for cars.
- The **Santa Luzia**, the inter-island ship, calling every second Thursday. In reality probably less often; fine for fiction.

## Daily life
- Maize bread; *inhame* (taro) from the wet fields; limpets off the rocks; *linguiça* at feasts.
- The Holy Ghost feasts (*Espírito Santo*) in late spring and June: the crown, the soup, the band.
- Women in black, men in caps.
- Paraffin lamps in the houses. No electricity in the village yet.
- The lighthouse has a radio set for the weather bulletins. Henrique can pick up Lisbon at night.
