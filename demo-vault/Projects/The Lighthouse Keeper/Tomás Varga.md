# Tomás Varga

The keeper. Elena's father. Born 1906, so 56 in 1962. Keeper at Ponta Negra since 1937, when he took over from his own father, the light's first keeper. **Going deaf** — and hiding it.

## The deafness
- Started around 1959, high sounds first. Now he misses voices from behind, the church bell, the radio, and the tick of the clock that turns the lens.
- He can't hear the fog horn any more. He **feels** it — palm flat on the wall of the horn house, counting the blasts through the stone. That image is the book.
- Reads lips. Turns his whole head when you speak, not just his eyes. Stands with his back to walls. Laughs half a second late.
- He used to wake before the clock weight touched the floor. That was his legend. Now Elena winds it.

## Why he hides it
A deaf keeper is not a keeper. If the inspector finds out, he's retired on half pay to a house in Santa Cruz with no sea in the window. The light is the only thing he's for, and the last thing he has of Amélia — they had their bed in the room under the lamp.

## How he is
- Speaks little and plainly. Short sentences, no flourishes.
- Keeps the log in a square, careful hand.
- Counts things: paraffin drums, mantles, days. Suspicious of the clerks in Horta.
- Used to play the viola da terra on the steps in the evening. Hasn't for two years. Undecided whether he plays again in Part Three.
- Loves his daughter in the form of instructions: "Twelve drums. Count them."

## Arc
From hiding the loss, to being found out (ch. 8 and 14), to teaching the relief keeper the clock by touch (ch. 17). Not a tragedy if I can help it: he loses the post and keeps the light some other way.

## Notes to self
- Never write him as pitiful. He's proud and a little frightening.
- He never narrates. We only see him from outside, the way Elena does. Ishiguro's lesson from [[The Remains of the Day]]: with a man like this, what he doesn't say is the point.
- He never names the deafness himself. Not once in the whole book.
