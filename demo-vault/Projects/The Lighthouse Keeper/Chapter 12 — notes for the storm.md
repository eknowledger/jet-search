# Chapter 12 — notes for the storm

The middle of the book. **A storm sinks the supply ship, and Elena keeps the light on alone for three nights.** Everything in Part One sets this up; everything in Part Three comes out of it. Target about 5,000 words — maybe more, maybe split in two.

## What has to happen
1. The night of 13 November. The *Santa Luzia* is coming from Horta to Flores for the last crossing before winter — the crossing Elena is booked on. Henrique is aboard.
2. The wind backs to the south-west and rises all afternoon. Henrique's last message (end of ch. 11, Morse, which Elena reads on the lighthouse set) says they'll be in by dawn.
3. Just after midnight: rockets off the point. A ship in trouble. The village men go down the cliff path with ropes. Tomás goes with them; Elena can't stop him.
4. Tomás falls on the rocks — leg broken, ribs. The men carry him to Graça's house. He can't climb the tower.
5. The ship is lost. Some of the crew come ashore alive; some don't. (Henrique: see below.)
6. The storm doesn't stop. **Elena keeps the light alone for three nights** — lamp, mantles, pressure, the clock every 2 hours 40, the horn when the rain turns to fog — with no sleep worth the name and nobody to relieve her.

## The question at the centre
Did the light fail? Early on the first night, before the rockets, Tomás was on watch. Did the clock run down while he sat there not hearing it? Did the *Santa Luzia* see a steady light instead of two flashes and think herself somewhere else?

- **Option A:** yes, and he knows. Too heavy — the book becomes about guilt.
- **Option B:** no, and everyone knows. Too light.
- **Option C (leaning):** nobody can ever know. The weight was low when Elena came up — five minutes from the floor, maybe less. Tomás believes it was his fault. Elena believes it wasn't. Neither can prove it. That's the wound for Part Three.

## Henrique
- **Dies:** honest, and it shuts the door to Lisbon in the cruellest way. Risk: he becomes a plot device.
- **Survives, injured, carried to Graça's:** gives Part Three a love story it doesn't need.
- **Missing for the three nights, alive in ch. 16:** Elena keeps the light not knowing. I like this best — the three nights are *about* not knowing. Decide before writing ch. 13.

## How to write it
- No weather adjectives in a row. Tell the storm through the tasks: the pressure gauge, the mantle that breaks, the crank, her hands.
- Night one: fear. Night two: the body — hunger, blisters, sleep. Night three: something like calm, almost joy. That's the dangerous part, and the true part: she's good at this.
- Count the windings. Forty turns each time. Let the reader count with her.
- Sound: she hears everything — the wind on the glass, the lens, the horn — and her father, down in the village, hears none of it.
- One short scene at Graça's: Tomás awake, palm flat on the wall, feeling the horn from a kilometre away. Too much? Try it and see.
