# Research — lighthouses of the Azores

Notes for the tower. Ponta Negra is invented, but it should feel like the real lights of the islands. Anything marked *(check)* I'm not sure of yet.

## Real lights worth knowing

| Light | Island | Why it matters to me |
|---|---|---|
| Ponta do Arnel | São Miguel | The oldest in the archipelago, lit in 1876. A steep road down; the keepers' houses huddled round the tower. |
| Ferraria | São Miguel | West coast, a lava field, hot springs in the sea below. |
| Capelinhos | Faial | Half-buried by the 1957–58 eruption; the ash came up to the windows. The eruption sent whole families to America. A keeper watching new land being born under his light — maybe Tomás knew him. |
| Albarnaz | Flores | The northwest tip of Flores; the nearest real light to my invented one. First lit in the 1920s *(check the year)*. |
| Lajes das Flores | Flores | The southern light, near the town of Lajes. |
| Rosais | São Jorge | On a cliff at the end of the island; abandoned after earthquake damage *(check: 1980?)*. |

## How a light of that time ran
- **The lens:** a big Fresnel lens, probably French-made, built from rings of glass prisms. It floats on a **bath of mercury**, so that a falling weight of a few kilos can turn a tonne of glass.
- **The clock:** the weight hangs on a chain down the hollow centre of the tower and drives the lens through gears. Someone has to wind it back up every few hours. In the book: every 2 hours 40 minutes, forty turns of the crank (invented, but plausible).
- **The lamp:** a paraffin vapour burner with an incandescent mantle. Pump up the pressure, preheat, light. Mantles are fragile; spares come on the supply ship.
- **The characteristic:** each light has its own pattern so ships know which one they're looking at. Ponta Negra: two white flashes every ten seconds. If the clock stops, the flashes stop and the light shows steady — the wrong signal. That's the danger at the heart of [[Chapter 12 — notes for the storm]].
- **Fog signals:** I'm not sure any Azorean light had a horn in 1962 *(check)*. Ponta Negra gets one anyway, from 1931: compressed air, an engine, and a flywheel that takes two people to start.
- **The log book:** times of lighting and extinguishing, weather, visibility, ships seen, stores used.
- **Mercury:** keepers handled it, topped it up, breathed it. Mercury poisoning was a real hazard — tremors, irritability. Could it be part of Tomás's deafness? Probably too neat. Keep it as something Graça suspects (ch. 6 and 18).

## Who ran them
Portugal's lights were run by the navy — today it's the Direção de Faróis *(check what it was called in 1962)*. In the book it's just "the Lighthouse Service in Lisbon". Keepers were civil servants, and an inspector came round once a year.
