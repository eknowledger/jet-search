# Writing habit

**600 words a day before breakfast. Sunday revision.** That's the whole system.

## Why before breakfast
By nine the day has opinions. Before breakfast nobody wants anything from me, the flat is quiet, and the part of my brain that judges sentences is still asleep. Evenings never held: I'd always be too tired, or out, or "just checking one thing".

## The morning
1. Alarm at 6:15. The phone stays charging in the hall.
2. Coffee. Pimenta gets fed first or there is no peace.
3. Sit down at the desk in the corner of the bedroom. (It used to be the kitchen table; the kitchen has been a building site since October — [[Kitchen Renovation]].)
4. Read the last paragraph from yesterday. Not more.
5. Write 600 words. No going back, no fixing, no looking things up — type [CHECK] and keep going.
6. Stop in the middle of a sentence if I can. It's much easier to start the next morning.
7. Write the count in the notebook. Breakfast.

It takes 40 to 70 minutes. On a good morning, 900 words; on a bad one, 600 words of nonsense. Both count.

## Sunday revision
Print the week's pages. Read them on the sofa with a red pen. Cut, mark, and rewrite the worst page by hand. Retype on Sunday afternoon. About a third of the raw words survive; the progress table on [[The Lighthouse Keeper]] counts only the survivors.

## Rules borrowed from [[Atomic Habits]]
- **Habit stacking:** after I pour the coffee, I sit down to write. Coffee is the trigger.
- **Never miss twice.** One missed morning is life; two is the start of a different habit.
- **Identity, not outcome.** I'm not "trying to finish a novel". I'm someone who writes before breakfast.
