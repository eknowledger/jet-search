# Elena Varga

The keeper's daughter. **19** in 1962 — born in March 1943 on a night of fog with the horn going ([[Chapter 7 — Dona Graça]]). She **wants to leave for Lisbon**, where her aunt has a room and a place at a nursing school waiting for her.

## What she wants, what she needs
- **Wants:** a city loud enough that nobody could ever ask her to listen for them. She imagines Lisbon as a sound, not a picture — all she has is a postcard of the Praça do Comércio and a newspaper photo of a bridge that hasn't been built yet.
- **Needs:** to choose. Leaving as escape and staying as duty are both ways of not choosing. The book is about the difference between leaving and being let go.

## What she knows that nobody else does
Since the spring she has been taking the first half of the night — winding the clock every two hours and forty minutes — because her father can no longer hear it slowing down. Some nights she keeps both halves. None of it is in the log book; the book still says *Tomás Varga, keeper*.

## How she moves
- Counts the ninety-six steps of the tower, every time. Her mother taught her; she can't stop.
- Faces her father when she speaks, slowly, so he can see her mouth. She has started doing it with everyone without noticing.
- Big hands like his, knuckles red from brass polish and cold water.
- Angry in bursts, then ashamed. Slams doors; apologises to objects.

## Voice
Close third, never inside her head for long. She notices sound — all the sounds he can't hear — so the prose around her is full of them. Let her be unfair sometimes. She's nineteen.

## Arc
1. **Fog** — carries the secret; plans to leave.
2. **Storm** — books the berth; then the ship that should carry her away sinks, and she keeps the light alone for three nights.
3. **Light** — has to decide, with nobody left to blame.

## Small things to use
- Her aunt's letter, folded small inside her shoe.
- She talks to the lens when she's alone. Calls it *a senhora*.
- Reads a little Morse: Henrique wrote the alphabet out for her on the back of an envelope, and she listens to the ships on the lighthouse set at night.
