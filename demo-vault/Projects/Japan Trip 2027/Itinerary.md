# Itinerary

**2027-03-28 → 2027-04-12** — 16 days, [[Rui]] and me. Bases: Tokyo (4 nights) → Kyoto (3) → Okayama (2) → Osaka (2) → Kanazawa (2, maybe) → Haneda (1), plus one night on the plane. The rule we agreed on in September: one big thing per day, never two, and a nap counts as a plan.

> "If we try to see everything, we'll remember nothing." — Rui, over the map, 2026-09-21

## Day 1 — Sunday 28 March: Lisbon → Istanbul

Clocks in Portugal go forward one hour at 01:00 this very night, so the alarm is set on the phone, not on the old clock in the kitchen. Taxi at 10:30; the flight to Istanbul leaves at 13:05 and lands at 19:55. Then five and a half hours in Istanbul airport: eat something proper, walk the long corridors, stretch the calves — the [[Half Marathon]] was exactly three weeks ago and they still remember it. The overnight flight to Haneda leaves at 01:30.

- Pimenta stays home; [[Sofia]] has the keys and the feeding schedule on the fridge.
- Sleep on the second flight, not the first. Tokyo is 8 hours ahead of Lisbon summer time.

## Day 2 — Monday 29 March: arrive in Tokyo

We land at Haneda at 19:10. Immigration, then the Suica cards (see [[JR Pass and Suica]]), then the Keikyu line, which runs straight onto the Toei Asakusa line — no change of train, about 45 minutes to Asakusa. Check in at [[Tokyo — Hotel Sakura Asakusa]] around 20:15.

- Dinner: something small and hot near the hotel — a bowl of ラーメン, or a convenience-store picnic in the room if we're wrecked.
- A short walk to Kaminarimon, then bed by 23:00.

## Day 3 — Tuesday 30 March: Tsukiji and the river

The time difference will wake us at 5 anyway, so we use it. Toei Asakusa line to Higashi-ginza and a short walk to the **Tsukiji outer market**. Breakfast at a sushi counter — the one Tokyo must-do (candidates in [[Restaurants to try]]). Then tamagoyaki on a stick and a careful look at the knife shops — the new kitchen deserves one good knife ([[Kitchen Renovation]]), but not on day 3.

- 10:30 Hama-rikyū gardens: early cherry trees, the teahouse on the pond, a bowl of matcha with a sweet.
- 12:30 the Tokyo Water Cruise boat from the garden pier up the Sumida river, back to Asakusa (about 35 minutes).
- Afternoon: nap. Non-negotiable.
- Evening: Sensō-ji after the crowds leave. Dinner: 天丼 at 大黒家, five minutes from the hotel.

## Day 4 — Wednesday 31 March: Ueno and Yanaka

Tokyo's own full bloom should be about now; Tokyo runs a few days ahead of Kyoto (see [[Cherry blossom forecast]]).

- 7:30 Ueno park, before the picnic tarps take over every square metre.
- Walk north to **Yanaka**: the cemetery avenue lined with cherry trees, Yanaka Ginza, cats on walls, old wooden houses that survived everything. This is the film day — the Olympus OM-1 with Portra 400, two rolls, nothing digital except the phone for maps.
- Lunch in Yanaka, then Nezu shrine and its tunnel of small red torii — a rehearsal for Fushimi Inari.
- Evening: Rui wants Shibuya at night. The crossing seen from above, then home on the Ginza line.

## Day 5 — Thursday 1 April: Shinjuku Gyoen

- 9:00 Shinjuku Gyoen (entry about ¥500): the widest spread of cherry varieties in Tokyo and big lawns to lie on.
- Lunch: tempura at つな八 in Shinjuku, at the counter to watch the frying.
- Afternoon: Meiji Jingū and the walk through its forest; Harajuku only if we want crowds.
- Evening: 焼き鳥 in Omoide Yokochō, one drink in Golden Gai if there's a free stool, then back to Asakusa to pack. Last night at the hotel.

## Day 6 — Friday 2 April: to Kyoto

JR Pass day 1 (valid 04-02 → 04-08). Exchange the order at Tokyo station and reserve seats for the whole week in one go. Hikari to Kyoto, about 2 h 40. Seats **E**, on the right side, for Mount Fuji — around 40 minutes out, if the sky is clear.

- Lunch: ekiben on the train.
- 15:00 check in at [[Kyoto — ryokan Mori-ya]]. Meet [[Kenji Mori]] at last, after weeks of emails. Shoes off, yukata on.
- 16:30 walk through Gion to the **Shirakawa canal** — cherry branches over the water, the postcard. Kenji's first tip.
- 18:30 kaiseki dinner at the ryokan. Bath at 21:30, bed.

## Day 7 — Saturday 3 April: Fushimi Inari at 6 am, full bloom

The day the forecast circles. Alarm at 5:00.

- 5:30 taxi (or the first JR Nara line train to Inari station) — **Fushimi Inari at 6 am**, the gates empty, the light coming in sideways through the orange. Walk up to the Yotsutsuji crossing for the view over the city; the summit loop is another hour, decide on the spot.
- 8:30 back at Mori-ya for breakfast, then sleep for an hour.
- 11:30 the **Philosopher's Path** (哲学の道) at full bloom, from Ginkaku-ji down to Nanzen-ji, slowly.
- Lunch: 湯豆腐 near Nanzen-ji.
- Afternoon: free. Rui reads; I take the X-T5 with the 23 mm and the "Lisbon Gold" recipe ([[Photography]]).
- Evening: 夜桜, night sakura, in Maruyama park — the big weeping cherry lit up. Dinner at the ryokan first.

## Day 8 — Sunday 4 April: Arashiyama

- Optional, legs permitting: a slow 5 km at dawn along the Kamo river. No watch.
- 7:00 JR to Saga-Arashiyama (on the pass). Bamboo grove by 7:30, before the tour buses.
- Tenryū-ji gardens when they open.
- Lunch by the Togetsukyō bridge.
- Afternoon: Nishiki market for snacks, a matcha parfait.
- Evening: last kaiseki at Mori-ya. Ask Kenji for one place nobody writes about, and write it down here.

## Day 9 — Monday 5 April: Himeji, then Okayama

- 9:30 check out and thank Kenji properly — the bottle of tawny port and the tile from Lisbon.
- Shinkansen to **Himeji** (about 50 min), bags into coin lockers at the station, walk up to Himeji castle — white walls, cherry trees around the moat.
- 15:00 on to **Okayama** (about 20 min). Check in at the station hotel.
- Evening: ramen and an early bed.

## Day 10 — Tuesday 6 April: Naoshima

Full plan in [[Naoshima art island]]. Tuesday on purpose: the Chichu Art Museum is closed on Mondays.

- 7:40 JR Uno line, Okayama → Uno (about 1 h, on the pass).
- Ferry from Uno to Miyanoura (about 20 min, not on the pass — cash).
- E-bikes at the port. Chichu Art Museum (timed ticket), lunch at its café, Benesse House Museum, the yellow pumpkin on the pier, the Art House Project in Honmura.
- Evening ferry back to Uno, train to Okayama, dinner near the station.
- Before bed: send the big bags to the [[Osaka apartment]] from the hotel desk (takkyūbin). Tomorrow is day packs only.

## Day 11 — Wednesday 7 April: Hiroshima and Miyajima → Osaka

The long day. Details in [[Hiroshima and Miyajima day trip]].

- 8:05 shinkansen to Hiroshima (about 40 min). Tram to the Peace Memorial Park and Museum. A quiet morning, on purpose.
- Lunch: Hiroshima-style お好み焼き, layered with noodles.
- JR to Miyajimaguchi and the JR ferry to Miyajima (both on the pass). Itsukushima shrine and the torii — check the tide table: high tide for the floating view, low tide to walk out to it.
- 18:30 shinkansen Hiroshima → Shin-Osaka (about 1 h 30). Into the apartment around 20:30, bags already there.

## Day 12 — Thursday 8 April: Osaka (last JR Pass day)

- Slow morning. Laundry — the apartment has a washing machine, our first since Lisbon.
- Option A: Nara in the morning (JR, on the pass) — Tōdai-ji, the deer, back for a late lunch. Option B: stay put and do nothing with great dedication.
- Afternoon: Kuromon market, then Osaka castle park, where the cherries are usually still holding on.
- Evening: Dōtonbori — たこ焼き at わなか, Osaka-style お好み焼き at 美津の, the neon, the canal. 串カツ if there's room.

## Day 13 — Friday 9 April: Kanazawa (maybe)

The decision point — see [[Kanazawa — maybe]]. If yes:

- Bags forwarded from the apartment in the morning; they arrive a day later, so the day packs carry one night.
- Thunderbird from Osaka to Tsuruga, then the Hokuriku shinkansen to Kanazawa, around 2 h 10 in total. Not on the pass — buy tickets the evening before.
- Afternoon: Higashi Chaya at dusk, the gold-leaf soft serve, an early dinner of Kanazawa おでん.

**Plan B (tired):** the Nozomi back to Tokyo, two quiet nights in Kuramae next to Asakusa, an onsen afternoon, a last look at Kappabashi, and nothing else.

## Day 14 — Saturday 10 April: Kenroku-en

- 7:00 Kenroku-en, which opens early in spring. Kanazawa castle park next door.
- Lunch: 海鮮丼 at Ōmichō market.
- Afternoon: the 21st Century Museum (Rui's pick — the swimming pool piece), then the Nagamachi samurai lanes.
- Evening: pack properly — one bag for the cabin, one for the gifts and the things we swore we wouldn't buy.

## Day 15 — Sunday 11 April: back to Tokyo

- Hokuriku shinkansen Kanazawa → Tokyo, about 2 h 30.
- Afternoon in Tokyo: Kappabashi for the knife (if it wasn't bought on day 3), a last 寿司 lunch.
- Night at an airport hotel at Haneda Terminal 3 — the flight is early.

## Day 16 — Monday 12 April: home

- 06:55 Haneda → Istanbul (13 h 10).
- 16:30 Istanbul → Lisbon, landing at 19:25.
- Pimenta will ignore us for an hour to make a point, then sleep on the suitcase.

## Rules for the trip

- Never more than one long train ride a day.
- If either of us says "tired", the next half-day is cancelled, no discussion.
- Cash for small places: ¥30,000 each to start, top up at 7-Eleven ATMs.
- Bags forwarded twice (Okayama → Osaka; Osaka → Kanazawa or Tokyo) so train days stay light.
- Calf raises every morning, 3×15, in every hotel room ([[Strength and mobility routine]]).
