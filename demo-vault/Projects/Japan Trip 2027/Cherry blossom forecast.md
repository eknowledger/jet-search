# Cherry blossom forecast

The whole trip hangs on one date: in **Kyoto, full bloom around April 3**. That's the long-range forecast (built on the ten-year average); the real forecasts start in January and update weekly in March.

| City | First bloom (est.) | Full bloom (est.) | We're there |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tokyo | ~03-24 | ~03-31 | 03-29 → 04-02 |
| Kyoto | ~03-27 | **~04-03** | 04-02 → 04-05 |
| Himeji | ~03-29 | ~04-04 | 04-05 |
| Okayama | ~03-27 | ~04-03 | 04-05 → 04-07 |
| Hiroshima | ~03-25 | ~04-02 | 04-07 |
| Osaka | ~03-27 | ~04-03 | 04-07 → 04-09 |
| Kanazawa | ~04-02 | ~04-07 | 04-09 → 04-11 |

Full bloom (満開, mankai) lasts about a week if there's no rain or strong wind. Then the petals come down — 花吹雪, a "petal blizzard", which is honestly just as beautiful.

## What it means for the plan

- **Kyoto 04-02 → 04-05** sits right on the peak. Fushimi Inari at 6 am is on 04-03, the Philosopher's Path the same afternoon.
- Tokyo will be at or just past full bloom — Ueno and Shinjuku Gyoen on 03-31 and 04-01.
- Kanazawa blooms later, so even when the south has dropped its petals, Kenroku-en should be at its best. One more argument for [[Kanazawa — maybe]].
- If the season comes early (warm winter): in Kyoto, do the sakura spots first and the temples later.
