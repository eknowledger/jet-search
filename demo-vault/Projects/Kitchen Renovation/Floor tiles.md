# Floor tiles

The kitchen floor is the one thing I want to keep: **terracotta tiles, 20 × 20 cm**, about **14 m²**, laid in the 1930s. Warm orange, a few darker ones, worn smooth in a path from the door to where the sink used to be. If at all possible they stay.

## What we have

| | |
|---|---|
| Tile | handmade terracotta, 20 × 20 cm, ~2 cm thick |
| Area | 14 m² (≈ 350 tiles) |
| Age | 1930s, same as the floor below |
| **Spares** | **2 boxes**, found in the storage room, left by the previous owner |
| Spares in tiles | 52, about 2 m² worth |

Two boxes isn't much, but it's enough to replace the broken ones and patch where the sink and the joist were.

## The plan

1. Keep the floor in place during demolition, under hardboard sheets taped at the joins
2. Only lift tiles where we have to (the old sink corner, the joist)
3. Clean lifted tiles, scrape off the old mortar, keep them numbered in order
4. Relay with a lime mortar, never cement, the old tiles need to breathe
5. Deep clean the whole floor at the end, then linseed oil and a beeswax finish

## What happened so far

- **2026-09-26**: lifted a loose tile by the door to look at the bedding: lime mortar over boards, good sign
- **2026-10-05**: floor covered before demolition started
- **2026-10-13**: lifted about 1.5 m² around the old sink, and found the rotten joist ([[Rotten joist under the sink]]). 6 tiles cracked on the way out
- **2026-10-23**: relaid over the repaired joist, using 9 tiles from the spare boxes (6 broken + 3 that were already chipped)

43 spares left. I'm keeping them forever.

## Care

> Terracotta drinks everything. Olive oil, red wine, beetroot. Wipe spills straight away until the oil finish has cured.

- Sweep, then a damp mop with a little neutral soap
- No vinegar, no bleach
- Re-wax once a year, maybe in October together with the boiler service ([[Home]])
