# Decision — induction or gas

**Decided 2026-09-03: induction.**

## Context

The old kitchen runs on gas: four rings from the 1980s, one of them dead, fed by the building's original pipes. With the room stripped back to the walls anyway, this is the one moment where switching costs almost nothing extra. [[Rui]] cooks on gas by instinct and wanted to keep it; I wanted to get rid of it. We agreed to write it down properly before arguing.

## Options

| | Gas (new hob) | Induction |
|---|---|---|
| Hob price | ~€450 | ~€600 |
| Extra cost | **€480 gas inspection** to sign off the installation | dedicated **32 A circuit** (already in the Ortega & Filhos quote) |
| Air in a small kitchen with one window | combustion fumes, needs a strong hood | nothing burns |
| Building pipes | the building's old gas pipes stay in use | gas capped at the meter |
| Wok cooking | real flame | flat-bottom wok, less drama |
| Cleaning | grates, burner caps | wipe a flat top |
| Pans | all fine | some old pans won't heat |

## Why induction

1. **Air quality.** The kitchen is 14 m² with one window onto the alley. Every article I read about gas cooking and indoor air said the same thing, and I don't want that next to the cat's bowl.
2. **The building's old gas pipes.** Luis was polite about them but said he would not want to be the one to connect a new hob to them.
3. **The €480 gas inspection** a new gas installation would need. The hob circuit is already in Luis's quote, so that money simply stays with us.

Rui's verdict, after a test week on a cheap portable induction plate: *"Fine. But I'm buying a carbon-steel wok and you're not allowed to complain about the price."*

## Consequences

- Electrician runs a dedicated 32 A circuit for the hob → [[Electrics and plumbing]]
- Gas capped at the meter; the gas contract gets cancelled (one less bill, see [[Finances]])
- Test every pan with a magnet; replace the ones that fail → [[Shopping list — kitchen]]
- Hood can be a smaller recirculating one; no gas means no minimum extraction rule
